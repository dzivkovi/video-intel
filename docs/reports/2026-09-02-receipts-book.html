The Receipts Book

Your corpus is a pile of transcripts. Three small stores are built from that pile, and each answers one kind of question. This page shows all three on one real subject, a curated topic called thinking-partner, using outputs generated 2026-09-02 against the live corpus, unedited.

The one picture

One pile, three derived stores

Every store below can be deleted and rebuilt from the markdown. That is what makes them cheap to trust: nothing in them is the only copy of anything.

Which creators actually cluster together?

python scripts/sdsm_network.py  ·  0.5 s

70 creators make 2,415 pairs. A naive overlap test flags 1,417 of them as connected. The degree-preserving null model prunes that to 51. Strongest tie in the corpus: a16z + southparkcommons, 4 shared concepts where 0.5 were expected (7.5x). Strongest among the big channels: mattpocockuk + ramjad, 44 vs 25.9 expected (1.7x).

Read the 51 as a shortlist of "these two are worth comparing side by side", never as a map of influence.

Discipline

How not to fool yourself

  • Small samples lie loudest. ramjad's lift of 6.11 rests on 3 firsts against 0.5 expected. Trust lifts backed by expected firsts of 5 or more (seankochel, mark_kashef); treat the rest as leads.
  • Upload date is not idea date. The ledger sees when something was published, never when it was first thought or said off-platform.
  • Corpus growth mimics topic fire. The volume table comes first in the burst report for exactly this reason.
  • A link is a claim. In the wiki, a [[link]] exists only where a lead-follow relationship was validated. Plain-text names are creators too small to rank.
  • The machine finds convergence; you supply the meaning. On a corpus this size every row is a lead for a human to inspect, not a verdict.
Explaining it to others

How to teach it in sixty seconds

  1. Transcripts are the asset; everything else is derived. Two thousand videos become markdown with timestamps once, paid once. Every store below is rebuilt from that markdown for free.
  2. Search finds the passage. LanceDB fuses keyword and meaning search and every hit opens the video at the second it was said. That is the "second brain" most people stop at.
  3. The ledger answers the questions search cannot. Who covered this first? Who follows whom? What is heating up right now? Which creators genuinely track the same ideas? Those are tables and timelines, not passages, so they live in DuckDB, beside the index, not on top of it.
  4. Every number carries a receipt. A claim points to a segment; the segment points to a quote and a timestamp. Rank is relevance, never truth; truth is the link you can click.
  5. The tool is willing to say "cannot tell". It corrects for corpus age and posting volume, prunes 1,417 naive ties to 51, and flags small samples. On a small corpus that modesty is the accuracy.
